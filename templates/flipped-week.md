# Flipped-week worksheet

Fill in one copy per teaching week (save it as `quality_reports/plans/YYYY-MM-DD_weekN-flipped-session.md`). The worked example is `quality_reports/plans/2026-10-13_week2-flipped-session.md`.

**Room constraint:** about 110 students, free seating. Every activity must work with **neighbour pairs** and **whole-room polls** (multiple choice, ranking or short text). Never depend on how groups are made up (for example "one expert per reading"): with free seating, that falls apart.

**Core rule:** every pre-class item must have a named point in the session where **not having done it costs the student something**. If an item doesn't, make that use happen, make the item optional, or cut it.

The question should be arguable, answered by the readings *and* the videos *and* the live material, and it shouldn't be a technical question.

| Item | Type | Mins / pages | What students bring in (the one thing) | Where it's used in class | Your story / margin note to add |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | video | | | block __ | |
| | reading | | | block __ | |

How to fill it in:
- **Bring in:** one prediction, one position, or one passage. "Having watched it" doesn't count.
- **Stories:** search the transcript for first-person anecdotes. Each video gets a one-line "Listen for…" on the session page that names the story, not the topic.
- **Margin notes:** extract your annotations (highlight + note per page). Pick 2–3 that *disagree with* or *extend* the author. These are your counterpoints in the plenary, and they show students how an expert reads.
- **Load check:** total the preparation time. If the videos run over about 50 minutes, or the readings total more than about 30 pages or more than 2 items, **split the readings by surname** (each student reads one closely and skims the rest). In class, bring the readings together through polls split by reading, not through mixed groups.

## Step 3: Turn comprehension questions into positions

Keep the existing study-guide questions as an optional self-check. For class, write **one shared question** that every reading can answer differently, plus "find one passage that proves it".

| Comprehension (self-check) | Position (used in class) |
|---|---|
| What are the six divisions of GDS? | Is "data science" new, a rebrand, or a bandwagon? Give one passage as evidence. |

## Step 4: Plan the session (120-minute pattern)

| Block | Mins | Purpose | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Deal + warm-up | 20–25 | Pays off the videos | Predict-the-output / spot-the-bug, in pairs, no laptops, then a poll. Take the snippets from the videos' own "Test Yourself" slides. Tell the story behind each one in 60 seconds or less. |
| 2. Reading activity | 30–35 | Pays off the readings | Poll "which reading did you prepare?" (anonymous, so you get a preparation rate) → poll each reading's verdict and show the results split by reading → 1 minute of silent writing (my position + one passage) → neighbours compare (a neighbour briefs anyone who didn't prepare) → short-text poll for a passage → plenary that starts where the readings disagree. In the plenary, **ask pairs or groups, not volunteers**: walk the room and ask "What did you two decide?", and ask which reading they used only *after* they answer. |
| Break | 10 | | |
| 3. Synthesis | 25–30 | The live material | Slides come from the live decks, but each segment is 10 minutes or less and **starts from something students said** in block 2. Build a bridge from a reading quote to the live topic. |
| 4. Make it concrete | 10 | Links to the practical | One live demo (code or command line) that connects the argument to Thursday's/the practical's code. |
| 5. Exit ticket | 5 | Feeds into next week | One sentence answering the week's question. Start at a **fixed time** (minute 110, or earlier if ahead), give 3 minutes of silence, and say it's anonymous. Quote a selection at the start of next week, so students see the tickets are used. |

Constraint: if the live decks hold more material than block 3 can take, **move it out** (to a short video, the practical, or a later week). Don't speed up.

If the session runs ahead of time, give the spare minutes to neighbour discussion, not to more lecturing.

**Participation (week 2 lesson):** with only volunteers, the same ~10% answered every time; asking a group worked. Every question to the room goes to a pair or group ("we decided…"), and each neighbour discussion starts with a minute of silent writing.

## Step 5: Communicate (send 2 days before)

The announcement should give:
- the week's question
- the reading split (by surname), if one is used
- "bring a phone" (for the polls)
- "Listen for" hooks, one per video
- what happens in the first 5 minutes (so students can see the preparation will be used)

## Step 6: Close the loop (after class, 5 min)

- [ ] Exit tickets collected → pick 3 for next week's opening slide
- [ ] Video analytics noted (before/after compared with last year)
- [ ] 3-line session log: what landed / what overran / what to change in this template
- [ ] Out-of-date references spotted in videos → `To_Do.md`

## Checklist before teaching

- [ ] Every preparation item appears in the "where it's used" column
- [ ] The session page has "Listen for" hooks and the reading split
- [ ] The poll deck is built (with a QR code on every poll slide), and the exit ticket is in the same export
- [ ] No activity depends on who sits where
- [ ] Every question to the room goes to a pair or group, not to a volunteer, and each neighbour discussion starts with a minute of silent writing
- [ ] No lecture segment runs longer than 10 minutes
- [ ] At least one margin note is shown in class
- [ ] Any promise made in a video ("let's discuss in class") is kept
